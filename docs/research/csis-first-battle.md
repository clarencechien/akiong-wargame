## 2. Four necessary conditions to defeat the invasion

| fact | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| Condition 1 | Taiwanese forces must hold the line / Taiwan must vigorously resist; "If Taiwan surrenders before U.S. forces can be brought to bear, the rest is futile" | CSIS p.3, p.83 |
| Condition 2 | No "Ukraine model": US must engage directly in combat within days with full capabilities; China can isolate the island "for weeks or even months"; Taiwan must start the war with everything it needs | CSIS p.3-4, p.83 |
| Condition 3 | US must be able to use its bases in Japan for combat operations; without them US fighter/attack aircraft cannot effectively participate | CSIS p.4, p.83 |
| Condition 4 | US must be able to strike the Chinese fleet rapidly and en masse from outside the Chinese defensive zone — i.e., enough air-launched long-range anti-ship cruise missiles (bombers + LRASM/JASSM-ER) | CSIS p.4, p.83 |

### PLA amphibious lift and buildup
| fact | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| Lift modeling method | lift = thousands of tons China can transport across the strait per 3.5-day turn; each battalion costs tons by type (leg inf, mech inf, armor, artillery, engineer); supply of forces ashore progressively reduces new lift | CSIS p.47 |
| Four delivery modes | amphibious landing over beaches (primary), air assault (helicopters, limited to extreme west of Taiwan), airborne (anywhere), captured ports/airfields | CSIS p.47-48 |
| Anticipated 2026 PLA amphibious fleet | 28 LSTs + 18 LHD/LPDs + 20 LSMs + 30 large civilian RO-ROs = 96 ships, serviced by 305 landing craft (vs. Normandy 547 ships + >3,000 landing craft) | CSIS p.108 |
| Troops ashore D-Day | ~8,000 on D-Day; ~16,000 in 3.5 days (vs. 90,000 Allied troops on D-Day 1944) | CSIS p.108 |
| Offload rate basis | base case uses late-WWII US offload rates (Operation Neptune/Normandy, Iceberg/Okinawa); excursion reduces lift 30% to match British Falklands rate | CSIS p.73-74 |
| Civilian lift caveat | credit given for purpose-built civilian RO-ROs; skepticism about small civilian craft for assault (Gallipoli analogy: 300-400 tons/day ≈ 1/50 of China's initial modelled offload capacity) | CSIS p.48 fn120; p.85 fn242 |
| Total PLA battalions landed — base | 37 landed; 30 remaining at end (≈30,000 personnel incl. non-combat); beachhead ≈2,600 km2 (7% of ~36,000 km2) | CSIS p.86-87 (Table 3) |
| Pessimistic | 60 landed; 43 remaining (≈43,000); 6,240 km2 (17%); supply at end: damaged ports/airports, air, sometimes a few ships | CSIS p.90 (Table 5) |
| Optimistic | 25 landed; 22 remaining (≈22,000); <1 hex (780 km2); air resupply only | CSIS p.93-94 (Table 7) |
| Taiwan stands alone | 230 landed over 2.5 months; 165 on island when Taipei fell (65 rendered combat-ineffective); ~300,000 personnel incl. non-battalion; whole island 36,000 km2 | CSIS p.97 (Table 9) |
| Taiwan-alone Chinese air support | 14 squadrons for ground support + 6 replacement, rest withheld to deter US/Japan | CSIS p.96; App. A p.150 |
| Landing location | 21 of 24 iterations landed in the south (lighter defenses); ~46% of Taiwan's battalions and half its mechanized forces are in the northern third | CSIS p.107-108 |
| Airborne forces | attempts to seize airfields "generally failed"; more useful isolating the battle area | CSIS p.107 |
| Penghu | captured in 3 iterations as staging base; "operational dead end" because amphibious capability dwindled | CSIS p.109 |
| Ammunition (Taiwan alone) | after 2 months fire rate halves; after 3 months artillery crews become infantry | CSIS p.96 |

## Not found / caveats
- No weather-window or seasonal constraint anywhere in the report.
- No absolute count of missiles fired in the opening strike; strike is pre-adjudicated and described qualitatively.
- No explicit total of Taiwanese active brigades in the running text (OOB appendices are not reproduced in text form in the PDF extraction beyond the sources cited).
- No explicit "days of supply" number; sustainment is tonnage-based per turn.
- Minor internal inconsistencies in the report itself: Chinese aircraft losses base = 155 (table) vs 161 (text); pessimistic Chinese ship losses 113 (table) vs 120 (text); optimistic Chinese aircraft 18 (table) vs 290 (summary p.101); US base aircraft 270 (table) vs 283 (summary p.101).
